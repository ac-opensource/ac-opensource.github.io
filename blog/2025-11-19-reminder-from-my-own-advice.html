Reminder I Didn’t Expect to Get From My Own Advice

On drifting into complacency, offering a friend honest advice, and realizing you need the same reminders.

Abstract

On drifting into complacency, offering a friend honest advice, and realizing you need the same reminders.

A friend reached out to me recently. He was shaken, confused, and unsure how things in his relationship slipped the way they did. I won’t share anything personal about him, but the conversation hit closer to home than I expected.

Because as I was telling him what I honestly believed he needed to hear, I caught myself thinking:

“Damn. I’ve been guilty of the same things.”

When relationships are new, effort comes naturally. You plan ahead. You prepare. You think about what the other person might need without being asked. You try to make things easier for them, not heavier.

Somewhere along the way, work, life, routine, stress, and comfort cause us to unintentionally slip.

Not in big dramatic ways, but in small, everyday ways that add up:

  • You pack at the last minute.
  • You forget something simple that matters to them.
  • You assume they’ll just understand.
  • You stop anticipating the little things.

You get too used to being loved, and because of that, you forget to show it back with the same level of effort.

I was telling him to pack early, plan his trip, think ahead for his partner, and it hit me like a quiet slap. I know that advice is right because I’ve also failed at it before. And sometimes I still do.

The hardest part is that these small oversights don’t make you a bad partner, they just make you human. But being human doesn’t erase the impact.

Taking someone for granted isn’t a decision. It's a drift.

It’s forgetting that the effort that feels minor to you might feel meaningful to them. It’s assuming “okay na yan” when it’s not okay anymore. It’s relying on comfort instead of intention.

And I realized that I’m not above any of this. I’m not some relationship guru. I’m just someone who has also gotten too comfortable at times, too distracted, too wrapped up in my own stress to notice when I stopped doing the things I used to do naturally.

What struck me was how easy it is to see what someone else is neglecting. It’s obvious when you’re on the outside looking in. But looking at yourself, that’s where it stings. Because you can’t pretend you don’t see the truth once it’s reflected back at you.

The advice I gave him sounded like this:

  • Think ahead.
  • Don’t wait for her to remind you.
  • Show effort without being asked.
  • Don’t be the cause of unnecessary stress.
  • Don’t assume she’ll always understand.

As I said those things, I realized: These reminders weren’t just for him. They were for me too.

Relationships don’t fall apart overnight. They fade when effort becomes optional. They weaken when appreciation becomes silent. They suffer when we forget that love isn’t maintained automatically. It’s maintained intentionally.

Talking to him made me remember to tighten up my own slack. To not wait until a problem becomes big. To take initiative again. To be proactive instead of reactive. To stop assuming “she knows how I feel” and actually show it.

Because the truth is simple but heavy: If you don’t want to lose something, don’t treat it like it can’t be lost.

Being there for my friend wasn’t just me supporting him. It was also life throwing me a reminder. That I shouldn’t wait for conflict to start doing the things I should’ve been doing all along. That consistency matters more than grand gestures. And that the person you love shouldn’t ever have to question whether they’re being taken for granted, especially when you actually do care.
